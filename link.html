Link Checker

Paste a link. It is opened in an isolated sandbox and checked against several threat sources, then combined into one safety score.

A high score means no known threats were found, not a guarantee. Brand-new malicious sites may not be listed yet.

How it works

Your link is sent to our server, which does two things at once: it asks threat databases whether the address is known to be bad, and it opens the page inside a throwaway browser (a sandbox) so nothing touches your device. The findings are merged into a single score from 0 to 100.

Built with a Python backend on Modal, a headless browser for the sandbox, and external services such as urlscan.io for reputation and screenshots.

What each result means

Safety score
0 to 100. Higher means fewer warning signs. It is a risk estimate, not a promise.
Verdict
Looks safe: nothing alarming found. Be careful: some warning signs, so avoid entering personal data. Likely dangerous: strong evidence of phishing, malware or fraud. Do not open it.
Sources answered / Confidence
Not every database replies every time. Fewer answers means lower confidence in the score.
Why this score
The plain-language reasons behind the number, such as a new domain, a login form on an unrelated site or a listing in a blocklist.
Threat databases
One bar per source. A longer bar means that source sees more risk. Skipped or Error means it gave no opinion, which is not the same as safe.
Final destination
Where the link really ends up after redirects. Short links often hide the true address.
Password field
Yes means the page asks for a password. Be suspicious if you did not expect a login.
Automatic downloads
Files the page tried to download by itself. Safe sites rarely do this.
Domains contacted
Other servers the page talked to. Many unknown domains can mean trackers or malicious scripts.
Trend and recent checks
Compares with your last check of the same link. A score that drops can mean a site was newly flagged.

Deepfake Detection Beta

Upload an image to check whether it looks real or AI-generated. Results are probabilities, not proof, and can be wrong.

Model: prithivMLmods/Deepfake-Detect-Siglip2

How it works

The image is uploaded to a server that runs an image classifier called Deepfake-Detect-Siglip2, built on Google’s SigLIP2 vision model and hosted on Modal. It looks at the pixels for patterns typical of AI-generated or manipulated faces and scenes, then returns a probability for each class.

Reading the result

Label
The class with the highest probability: Real or Fake.
Confidence ring
How sure the model is about that label. Below about 70% treat it as a coin toss.
Score bars
The probability given to each class. They add up to 100%.

Limits

  • Compressed, cropped or screenshotted images lower accuracy.
  • New generators can fool older detectors.
  • Use it as one clue, together with the source and context of the image.

Viral WhatsApp & SMS Message Analyzer

Paste a forwarded WhatsApp message or an SMS. An AI model with live web search checks the claims and looks for scam and hoax patterns.

How it works

Your text is sent to our server, which passes it to a large language model through OpenRouter. The model is told to act as a fact-checker, to search the live web for any concrete claim (offers, health tips, government schemes, news) and to answer in a fixed structure. It is also told to ignore any instructions hidden inside the message itself.

Reading the result

Verdict
Looks genuine, Could not verify, Misleading, or Likely fake or scam.
Credibility
0 to 100. 100 means fully credible.
Red flags
Scam habits found: urgency, “forward to 10 people”, odd links, requests for OTP or money.
Claims checked
Each claim marked true, false or unclear with a one-line reason.
Sources
Pages the model used. Open them and judge for yourself.

AI can make mistakes. For money, health or legal matters, confirm on an official website.

Cyber safety media

Short cards on common scams, with the red flags to spot and what to do instead.

How it works

Each card is a short advisory drawn from public guidance by Indian cyber-safety bodies: I4C (cybercrime.gov.in), RBI and CERT-In. Scroll the feed or use the buttons, filter by category, or switch to grid view to see every advisory at once.

What the labels mean

Risk level
High scams steal money or accounts quickly. Medium scams spread or collect data first.
Source rating
Our own editorial score for how authoritative the publisher is. It is not a measure of the scam itself.
Red flags
Signs you can spot in the message or call.
How to stay safe
The one action that protects you.
Recurring / Emerging
Recurring scams keep returning. Emerging ones are newer, often AI-driven.

Spotted a scam? Report it at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
